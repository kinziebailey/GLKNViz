Introduction

The Inventory & Monitoring (I&M) Division provides science to National Park managers, so they can make sound, science-based management decisions to conserve America’s most treasured places. The Great Lakes Inventory and Monitoring Network (GLKN) is part of the I&M and conducts long-term ecological monitoring in the following parks: Apostle Islands National Lakeshore, Grand Portage National Monument, Indiana Dunes National Park, Isle Royale National Park, Mississippi National River and Recreation Area, Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore, Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore, St. Croix National Scenic Riverway, and Voyageurs National Park. GLKN’s inventory and monitoring work provides park managers with status and trends data on vital signs that represent the health of natural resources, including lake, river, and stream water quality in most parks in the network.

Purpose

The Water Quality Portal (WQP) is a data portal that provides access to publicly available discrete water-quality data. This visualizer tool uses data GLKN publishes on the WQP so users can easily explore, analyze, and download water quality data for parks in the network. More information on GLKN's water quality monitoring can be found in the About tab.

The analytical tools and comparisons to ecologically meaningful thresholds provided in this visualizer are for data exploration purposes only. Any noteworthy trends or data that commonly exceed thresholds would require further exploration and confirmation using rigorous analytical techniques, with findings subjected to scientific peer review.

Basic Operation

To run the visualizer, follow these basic steps:

  1. Review this Instructions and About tabs
  2. Select Park of interest
  3. Select one or more Site(s) of interest
  4. The map will zoom to the last Site selected
  5. Select the Figure of interest from the tabs at the top
  6. Select a Parameter(s) to plot
  7. Select Year(s) of interest

Additional Features

  1. Each figure type has an associated About button. When clicked, a popup window will appear with information specific to each figure
  2. To download a figure or make full screen, hover the mouse above the figure and buttons will become visible in the upper right corner. Select the button that corresponds to the action you are interested in.
  3. Hovering the mouse over single data point (or boxplot) on a figure will create a small pop-up window with information about that data point (or boxplot).
  4. Information about the total number of measurements plotted and number of censored data points are shown above each figure. The numbers for the different measurement categories are a function of the time period selected.
  5. Ecologically meaningful, parameter specific thresholds can be plotted by clicking the box next to Thresholds. The Exceedances table below each figure contains references and descriptions of threshold values. Note: Threshold values can be state and site-specific and may change depending on the park and site of interest.
  6. Linear, locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS), and polynomial regression types can be selected for Time Series and Correlation figures.
  7. Data associated with each figure are shown in the Figure Data tab below each figure. Data plotted in the figure determine what values are displayed in the Figure Data tab. The same is true for the Exceedances tab.
  8. Data can be copied or downloaded as a .csv or .xlsx file by clicking on the buttons on the top left of either table.

General Notes

  1. All data in this visualizer are fully QA/QC’d and available to the public.
  2. Data are added to the visualizer annually, with a lag time of approximately one-year from the current year, to allow for full data QA/QC and publication.
  3. To be included in the visualizer, a site must have at least five years of data available or be actively monitored by GLKN.
  4. Values reported by analytical laboratories that fall under the lower quantification limit (also commonly referred to as < reporting limit or < method reporting limit) but above the detection limit are included as data points in the figures but should be treated with caution. Those values are less accurate than values at or above the lower quantification limit. Values that were below the detection limit as reported by laboratories are not included in the visualizer but the total number of data points meeting that condition are reported above the figure.
  5. Not all parameters are available for every site. For example, water clarity is measured with total suspended solids at river sites, while Secchi disk depth is measured at lake sites.
  6. Secchi disk depth, bottom depth, and water level are physical measurements not associated with collection of a water sample.
  7. Water samples are collected near the surface in the upper 2 meters of the lake, river, or stream.
  8. The Profile Figures tab only shows water quality parameters measured with a sonde, from the surface to the bottom of a water body.
  9. The Time Series, Boxplot, and Correlation Figures tabs only show data collected in the upper 2 meters, except for parameters collected as physical measurements.
