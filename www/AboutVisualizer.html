Disclaimer

This visualizer tool allows users to explore discrete water quality data collected by The Great Lakes Inventory and Monitoring Network (GLKN). It uses data GLKN publishes in the interagency Water Quality Portal (WQP), a public-facing data repository that allows users to search, download, and consume consolidated water-quality data. GLKN water quality data in the WQP is identical to that published on the National Park Service (NPS) Data Store, where GLKN water quality data are published as data packages, found here: NPS Data Store. The analytical tools and comparisons to ecologically meaningful thresholds provided in this visualizer are for data exploration purposes only and are not intended for direct application in regulatory or enforcement activities. This tool should not be used in the place of rigorous hypothesis testing, quantitative analysis, or weight-of-evidence approaches to decision-making. Any noteworthy trends or data that commonly exceed thresholds would require further exploration and confirmation using rigorous analytical techniques, with findings subjected to scientific peer review.

Introduction

The NPS preserves and protects the natural and cultural resources of the National Park System for the enjoyment, education, and inspiration of current and future generations. The NPS also cooperates with partners to extend the benefits of natural and cultural resource conservation and outdoor recreation throughout this country and the world.

GLKN is part of the NPS Inventory and Monitoring Division of NPS, which conducts science in national park units. They oversee long-term monitoring of natural resources at nine National Park Units in the western Great Lakes Region: Apostle Islands National Lakeshore (APIS), Grand Portage National Monument (GRPO), Indiana Dunes National Park (INDU), Isle Royale National Park (ISRO), Mississippi National River and Recreation Area (MISS), Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore (PIRO), Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore (SLBE), St. Croix National Scenic Riverway (SACN), and Voyageurs National Park (VOYA). GLKN conducts water quality monitoring at most of these parks with a goal to understand the health and condition of aquatic ecosystems and water resource in monitored waters. More specifically, GLKN’s objective is to monitor basic limnological parameters that describe the water quality of select inland lakes and rivers to describe the current status and trends of these waterbodies.

The analytical tools and comparisons to ecologically meaningful thresholds provided in this visualizer are for data exploration purposes only and are not intended for direct application in regulatory or enforcement activities. This tool should not be used in the place of rigorous hypothesis testing, quantitative analysis, or weight-of-evidence approaches to decision-making.

Sampling Locations

As of August 2026, GLKN is actively monitoring water quality at 30 inland lake and 8 river sites across five national parks. Inland lakes are monitored at Apostle Islands (APIS), Pictured Rocks (PIRO), and Sleeping Bear Dunes (SLBE) national lakeshores and Isle Royale (ISRO) and Voyageurs (VOYA) national parks. The river sites are located at St. Croix National Scenic Riverway (SACN). Since the onset of monitoring in 2006, GLKN has added monitoring at some sites and suspended monitoring at others, and suspended monitoring altogether at Mississippi National River and Recreation Area (MISS) and Indiana Dunes National Park (INDU) due to duplication of effort with other agencies or other factors.

All changes in monitoring are fully described in our water quality monitoring protocols. GLKN has also monitored water quality at additional sites as part of shorter-term projects or opportunistically when staff time and funding are available. To be included in the current iteration of this visualizer a site must be either actively monitored or have at least five years of water quality data.

Sampling Frequency

GLKN began monitoring water quality in 2006 and monitoring is ongoing. At inland lakes, water quality is monitored three times in the summer, generally in June, July, and August, with a few parks monitoring in May or September. For the river sites, water quality is monitored monthly during ice-free conditions, April – November. Some data are missing as a result of missed sampling or extenuating circumstances, such as bad weather or federal government shutdowns. Some parameters are monitored more frequently than others, as outlined below.

Parameters

As of August 2026, GLKN monitors the 21 water quality parameters listed below. While the current iteration of the visualizer only contains data on these 21 parameters, additional information may be included in the future. GLKN measures six parameters during every site visit to lakes (three times a annually) and rivers (eight times annually). Additional parameters (8 - 21 below) are measured less frequently (lakes: one time annually, rivers: three times annually), which provides a more robust understanding of changes through time. Two notable exceptions are the suspended monitoring of cations (Ca, Mg, Na, K) at most inland lakes from 2013-2018 due to financial constraints and the increase in monitoring of dissolved organic carbon around 2013 to three times annually at most lakes.

Data Quality

The overall objective of quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) is to ensure that data generated by a project are meaningful, representative, complete, precise, comparable, scientifically defensible, and reasonably free from bias. As such, GLKN water quality monitoring QA/QC procedures are extensive, beginning with field preparations, through the collection of data, to the final analysis and reporting of results. Each water quality monitoring protocol has a unique standard operating procedure that describes all aspects of GLKN’s water quality QA/QC. These procedures can be found here:

Standard Operating Procedure #12: Quality Assurance/Quality Control, version 1.3

Standard Operating Procedures for Large Rivers Water Quality Monitoring: Great Lakes Network, version 1.1

When samples are sent to laboratories for analysis, those samples undergo the laboratories own QA/QC procedure. Reports are generated with all results and data that do not meet laboratory QA/QC requirements are flagged and censored.

As part of GLKN's annual data submission process, NPS Water Resources Division checks the data to ensure it is formatted properly, does not contain grammatical errors, and compares it against prior submissions to document any edits to historical data. Additionally, GLKN is in the process of developing an automated reporting process to compare current year data to historical data to facilitate internal review and analysis. More information about this process will be included here in the future.

In the current iteration of the visualizer, values reported by analytical laboratories that fall under the quantification limit (also referred to as “below reporting limit” or “below method reporting limit”) are included as data points in the figures. We are confident that these values are close approximations to actual values. However, they should be treated with caution, particularly when doing rigorous statistical analyses. Values that were under the detection limit as reported by laboratories are not included in the visualizer but the total number of data points meeting that condition are reported above the figure.

More on Thresholds

Under the Clean Water Act, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency establishes water-quality criteria through scientific evaluations of ecological conditions and human health risks. It recommends these criteria to states and tribes, enabling them to develop water‑quality standards that serve as the basis for regulating pollutant discharges.

This visualizer uses the term “threshold” to broadly encompass criteria, standards, or thresholds to indicate when a parameter may be at levels detrimental to water quality. These threshold values can be state and site-specific, which is reflected in the visualizer. The Exceedances table contains references and links for more details on thresholds when available. These thresholds are provided to contextualize the data and when appropriate, lead to further investigation of a parameter if levels are approaching or exceeding a threshold.
